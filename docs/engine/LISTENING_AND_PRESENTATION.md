# Engine | Listening Output and the UI Projection

## Shared finalizer
Common BGM and existing Instrumental normal-use paths should share validated audio integrity, loudness/true-peak policy, ReplayGain, metadata ownership, source/provenance, atomic saving, missing-output repair and restart reuse. Reuse should not silently alter PCM or force GPU separation merely because a tag or OS dependency changed.

Tags, covers, ReplayGain and music-library information are durable Product state, not disposable implementation state; new official metadata updates them through the same revision record as any other LSS-owned result. No placeholder output folder when there is nothing to save.

## Music-library metadata (user decisions, 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22)
LSS itself acquires, reconciles and decides this metadata at runtime from public sources; no developer, agent or user looks anything up. The internal record is the authority: every fetched raw value with its source, every disagreement, name history, occurrences and relations stay inside LSS; tags are a projection of the resolved values, and only values that are obtained and readable as meaning are written (no hashes, recipe ids or provenance JSON in tags).

- **Album = one series.** TV series and movies are different albums; specials and recap episodes belong to their TV series. A franchise name never decides the series: the series is the one whose own article lists the episode, and an official soundtrack matches by composer and a release on or after the series start. LSS output joins the official soundtrack album: the album title is the official title with volume marks (`Vol.1`, `vol.2`, `第1弾` …) removed, or `<series> オリジナル・サウンドトラック` when removal leaves something unnatural or no soundtrack exists yet.
- **Discs and tracks:** official discs keep their order and continue across volumes; pieces on no official disc go on one further disc. `DISCNUMBER` is always written; `TOTALDISCS` is not. A cue of an officially released recording (edit, TV version, component variant) takes that recording's official disc/track; unreleased pieces are numbered on their disc in broadcast order (first-use episode, then time); processing an earlier episode later may renumber them. `TRACKNUMBER` is separate from `M_NUMBER`.
- **Date:** one per album: the TV series' first broadcast date, or the movie's release date; never a soundtrack release or video upload date.
- **Titles:** a verified official title, else LSS's provisional title (below); a fan site's provisional name is never copied. Source ranking for every field: MusicBrainz, VGMdb, Wikipedia, then distribution catalogues. A store value never outranks a booklet-faithful source, and store-added suffixes such as ` (Instrumental)` are removed; the raw value stays internal.
- **Credits:** every role a source provides is written per track, each role in its own tag named by the source's English role (`COMPOSER`, `ARRANGER`, `FLUTE`, `CONDUCTOR`, `ORIGINALCOMPOSER` …; Japanese-only role words map through a fixed table), album-level credits to every track they cover, track-level ones only to those tracks. Nothing is inferred. `ARTIST` and `ALBUMARTIST` are not written, nor catalogue number or label.
- **One tag, one meaning.** Occurrences are `USE_EPISODE`, `USE_START`, `USE_END`, `USE_SCENE` (parallel multi-values; observed scenes only) and `FIRST_USE_EPISODE`, `FIRST_USE_DATE`; relations are one tag per kind (`EDIT_OF`, `COMPONENT_VARIANT_OF`, `ADDITIONAL_MATERIAL_ON`, `ARRANGEMENT_OF`, `SHARES_MOTIF_WITH`, value = the related title); `COMPLETENESS`, `REPRESENTATION`, `SERIES_MUSIC` (the series' staff music credit, not a claim about this piece). No `RELATION` or `VERSION` umbrella tag.
- **Versions are open-ended, one tag per part or aspect:** `<PART>_VER` with an English value, from whatever part LSS observes (vocals, chorus, bass, drums, piano, strings, guitar, synth …) or aspect (`TV_VER=1`, `MOVIE_VER`, `LOOP_VER=Cut`, `INTRO_VER=Only`, `TAKE`). Values are `Only` / `Cut`; presence is the default and not written; written only when observed. The `_VER` suffix keeps them apart from credit tags (`PIANO=` performer vs `PIANO_VER=`).
- **M number and order sheet:** `M_NUMBER` is LSS's number for an unreleased piece: a work prefix plus a number (`AB01`; a new season from `AB101`); verified versions of one piece share it with a letter suffix (`AB05a`, `AB05b`, a fan-database convention). The prefix is unique within the series, so works of one franchise never share it (not one prefix for every series of a franchise): it comes from the work's own first subtitle and soundtrack title. A piece with a known official M number is released and is not extracted. `ORDER_MENU` (menu / use), `ORDER_DIRECTION` (musical direction) and `M_SECTION` (booklet-like heading) are separate tags. Two fan databases are the design references for these conventions; neither is bundled.
- **File name:** `{disc, 2 digits}.{track, 3 digits}.{title}[ - {composer}][ [{version}]][ [{M number}]].flac`, e.g. `01.001.Title - Composer [M-36]`. The composer appears only when obtained; the version is a display form such as `Anime Ver.`. A rename never creates a new cue; it is a recorded naming revision.
- STEM and INSTRUMENTAL outputs follow the same tag rules.
- VGMdb blocks non-browser clients; LSS reads archived copies of VGMdb album pages (Internet Archive) and never impersonates a browser or solves challenges.

## Identity versus title
An official title is preferable when verified; a known original in an anime version says so. Otherwise the provisional title is a real, song-like name, marked provisional, **written in the manner of the work's own official titles**: as soon as LSS knows even one verified official track title of the work (its soundtrack, a theme single, a movie of the same work), that title set is the style guide — language (an English-titled work gets English-style names), length, grammatical form, vocabulary, punctuation and mood — and the new name should read like a track that could sit on that album (user, 2026-10-22, repeating an earlier request). Creative and poetic freedom lives inside that manner. This applies only when an official title is known. A work with no released soundtrack yet, or whose titles are unknown, is a separate case the user has not specified yet (user, 2026-10-22); until it is, the Japanese fan provisional-title manner for unreleased BGM (the fan usage-list style) stays as before, and it is not to be presented as the settled rule. The style is learned from the titles as data (no per-work rules written into the Product), recorded with its source, and a style learned later may yield a better name, which replaces the LSS-owned one under the replacement rule below. It is synthesized from every clue LSS can measure or verify — the **scene** the music played in, from observed evidence (dialogue transcript, subtitles, video, events) kept apart from inference, and the **music itself**: tempo, beat, metre, rhythm pattern, instrumentation and roles (including separated stems), structure, melody, harmony/key, timbre, development and direction (bright, dark, tense, gentle…) — like a score order sheet: this kind of scene, this kind of music. A song whose scene is unknown is named from the music (user, 2026-10-16 late session and 2026-10-18; reading the earlier correction as a ban on music-derived names is a misreading). The name is an expression, not a list: `ピアノ／ニ長調／120 BPM` is description metadata and search tags, not a title. Sound alone never invents a scene event or character; poetic musical expression is free, factual scene claims need evidence. Naming reuses existing analyses and stems; it never reruns heavy separation just to name. LSS's own M-number-like grouping and variation labels (A/B, component-muted versions) are separate metadata, never pushed into TITLE or TRACK (a filename may carry them). **LSS-owned results are the current LSS's best:** extracted audio, cue boundaries, the provisional title and LSS-owned tags are replaced when a newer LSS produces a better result; each replacement is recorded as a naming revision on the CurrentBest lineage, and the previous audio and name stay recoverable through history and rollback. **One musical identity, one name:** every occurrence LSS recognises as the same piece (the same recording or track identity, recurring across episodes or within one) carries the same title at any moment, and distinct pieces never share a title. A better name from a newer LSS, or a verified official title, replaces the old one for the whole identity at once, across all its occurrences and recorded in the naming revision; one identity under two titles is a defect. Re-running the same LSS version on the same input changes no name and no result (no churn without a better result). Starting the application resumes only naming that a job the user started has not finished (a cue never attempted, or a transient failure due for retry with unchanged inputs); a newer model, policy or public information makes an old source's unresolved name a reprocess candidate, renamed when the user names that source again or starts a reprocess, never by a launch on LSS's own initiative. When the scene cannot be identified, no scene is invented (the music-derived name stands; work, episode and time stay as the reference); scene evidence may be added later when evidence arrives, without changing identity. A misheard line is not a fact; an interpretation ("X's theme") is not asserted as fact, and never from a single occurrence. A cue used in several scenes has one representative title; its other scenes stay recorded. Missing scene knowledge never blocks or delays extraction. Titles and file paths do not create a new Cue/Asset. A rename cannot masquerade as new audio. Keep cases of restoration/rebuild/reuse/improvement distinct.

## Work music menu
LSS derives a per-work menu from musical use, cue groups and variations, not from processing order: cues of one piece share an M-number-like base, variations are distinguished as A/B (as `WF04A`/`WF04B` illustrate a direction, never a per-work table in the Product), and version differences such as instrument-dropped or stem-dropped versions and alternate arrangements are kept as separate version metadata. The grouping and numbering are LSS's own provisional organisation, are re-derived when episodes are processed out of order, are never pushed into TITLE or TRACK, and never change a permanent cue identity.

## Interface grammar
- Three states: READY -> PROCESSING -> COMPLETE. Progress shows measured progress or an honest indeterminate state, never an invented percentage.
- READY action: **START**.
- COMPLETE heading: **SESSION COMPLETE**; auto-save is normal.
- User requests semantic intent; model/Recipe selection is internal. Three result boards in this order (keys 1/2/3): BGM, INSTRUMENTAL and STEM; STEM offers instrument and voice chips (no free text), each a single stem or part of an `A+B` combination, frozen at START for every SOURCE that wants STEM. Each wanted stem reports per item (saved, not found) (`WANTED_SOUND.md`). Japanese UI text uses no middle dots or colons as separators.
- Persona 5 Royal-inspired strong hierarchy (not a literal copy): red action, cyan processing, gold results.
- Concise English anchors with Japanese explanatory and result text. The English-only *engineering-document* rule does not translate away actual Japanese UI content.
- No routine manual download or open-folder task and no menu/zoom controls without renewed Product reason.
- No fake controls: a button, menu, progress or status that does nothing real is never shown as a finished feature; prototype-only state is marked as a Development artifact and never reaches the production UI.
- An error says what failed, whether existing results are safe, what LSS does next and whether the user must act; raw logs are never the primary error experience (structured diagnostics stay internal).
- Fidelity is checked on the real application on the target display, never on a canvas, preview or design handoff alone: geometry, text metrics, scaling, transform origin, timing, easing, clipping, hit areas and state behavior are measured against the reference screenshots (and video where motion matters), state by state, so a later state's polish never hides an earlier state's miss. Subjective visual or tactile fit between measurably equal implementations belongs to the user's design intent, not to the implementing agent.
- When the same state keeps missing the reference without a new technical cause, stop revising prose and run a bounded representative proof of concept to re-evaluate the renderer/presentation architecture.
- Existing precise motion/impact rules remain Product design data where implemented; preserve confirmed behavior rather than rewriting from a generic animation template.

## Projection must match state
Published, already available, reused, improved, rebuilt, restored, held, unsupported, absent, failed, and unknown are different. The COMPLETE screen and any saved report are projections of Product state, not secondary decision engines. UNKNOWN counts must never be shown as zero without evidence.
